1. Wages and Working Hours
Minimum Wage & Overtime: Employees are legally entitled to receive at least the standard federal minimum wage for all hours worked. Any non-exempt employee working more than 40 hours in a single 168-hour workweek must be paid overtime compensation at a rate of at least 1.5 times their regular pay rate.  
Recordkeeping & Hourly Tracking: Employers must maintain accurate records of hours worked and total compensation paid for all employees.  
Worker Classification: Employers must accurately classify individuals as employees or independent contractors. Independent contractors do not receive statutory protections under standard wage laws.  
Key Enforcing Agency: U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) – Wage and Hour Division (WHD).  
Primary Governing Statute: Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).  
2. Workplace Health and Safety
Safe Working Environment: Every employee has the right to work in an environment free from known physical, health, and safety hazards.  
Hazard Training & Information: Employers must provide safety training, equipment, and access to material safety data in languages and terms employees can understand.  
Reporting Safety Violations: Workers have the right to request an official inspection and file a complaint regarding unsafe working conditions or hazards without fear of job loss or employer punishment.  
Key Enforcing Agency: Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA).  
Primary Governing Statute: Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSH Act).  
3. Equal Opportunity & Anti-Discrimination
Protected Classes: Employers cannot make employment decisions (hiring, firing, promotions, wage setting, work assignments) based on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy and sexual orientation), national origin, age (40 or older), disability, or genetic information.  
Equal Pay: Men and women in the same workplace performing substantially equal work must receive equal pay for equal work.  
Reasonable Accommodations: Employers must provide reasonable workplace accommodations for qualified employees with disabilities or sincere religious beliefs, provided it does not cause an undue hardship for the employer.  
Key Enforcing Agency: Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC).  
Primary Governing Statutes:  
Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964  
Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)  
Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA)  
Equal Pay Act of 1963  
4. Protected Family & Medical Leave
Job-Protected Unpaid Leave: Eligible employees who have worked for a covered employer for at least 12 months are entitled to take up to 12 workweeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year for specific family or medical reasons.  
Qualifying Reasons: Childbirth/newborn care, adoption or foster care placement, caring for an immediate family member with a severe health condition, or treating the employee’s own serious health condition.  
Benefit Maintenance & Reinstatement: Employers must maintain employee health benefits during leave and reinstate the employee to their original or equivalent position upon return.  
Key Enforcing Agency: U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) – Wage and Hour Division (WHD).  
Primary Governing Statute: Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). 

5. Right to Organize & Protection Against Retaliation
Collective Action & Unionization: Employees have the legal right to organize unions, join labor organizations, engage in collective bargaining, or act together with coworkers to improve working conditions.  
Whistleblower & Anti-Retaliation Rights: Employers are strictly prohibited from penalizing, firing, demoting, harassing, or retaliating against any employee for exercising their legal labor rights, filing a complaint, reporting illegal activity, or participating in an investigation.  
Key Enforcing Agency: National Labor Relations Board (NLRB).  
Primary Governing Statute: National Labor Relations Act (NLRA).
